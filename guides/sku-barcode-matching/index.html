SKU and barcode matching: get the right variants connected

Match the exact variant, handle duplicate identifiers, and review product pairs before you sync.

By Meridian AppsOctober 9, 20265 min read

  • Use consistent identifiers across stores

The variant is the inventory item

Good Shopify inventory mapping starts with the exact variant. Two stores can use different product titles and still sell the same item. Equally, two variants can share a product title while representing different sizes, colors, or materials.

A product named “Everyday Tee” might have six variants. Matching the title alone cannot tell you whether Small / Black belongs with Small / Black or Large / Navy. SKU and barcode identifiers are useful because they can identify the specific variant across your stores.

Use consistent identifiers across stores

StockBridge can find matching variants by SKU or barcode. Decide which identifier your team maintains consistently, then check the actual values in both Shopify catalogs. For a normal one-to-one setup, one source variant should correspond to the intended destination variant.

Example pair What to check
TEE-BLK-S → TEE-BLK-S Same item, black color, small size. Confirm the barcode and options too.
TEE-BLK-S → TEE-BLK-L Different size. A similar title does not make this a valid inventory pair.
One SKU on several variants Ambiguous identity. Review every candidate rather than selecting the first match.
No SKU or barcode Auto-detection has no useful identifier to compare. Add an identifier or review a manual mapping.

Keep an identifier consistent in spelling and format across stores. If a result does not look right, inspect the stored SKU and barcode rather than relying on what a product title suggests.

Treat duplicate identifiers as a review step

A repeated SKU or barcode can point to more than one destination variant. That might be intentional in your catalog, or it might be a data-entry issue. Either way, it does not tell the app which inventory relationship your business wants.

The “Allow duplicate SKUs” option is relevant to automatic matching. Use it only when you understand why the identifier is repeated and have reviewed the pairs it creates. It is not a setting that automatically makes all duplicate variants represent the same physical stock.

Review both sides. For bidirectional sync, ask which variant is allowed to change the other variant’s Available quantity. Multiple writers targeting the same inventory item can make the intended outcome unclear.

Different connections can reuse a variant

A product can be involved in separate store connections. For example, Retail ↔ Wholesale and Retail → Regional are different relationships, so reusing the retail variant is not automatically a duplicate error.

Review each connection independently, and then review the network as a whole. Record the direction, variant pairs, and mapped locations for each relationship. Avoid assuming that a setting or mapping in one connection also controls another.

A checklist before enabling a mapping

  1. Confirm the connection. Check which source and destination stores are selected.
  2. Check the variant options. Compare size, color, material, and any other option that changes the item.
  3. Compare identifiers. Check the SKU and barcode in both Shopify admins.
  4. Review all candidates. Investigate repeated identifiers or several variants pointing at one inventory item.
  5. Confirm location and direction. Product identity alone does not establish the warehouse or the stock owner.
  6. Verify a real update. Compare Available quantities and inspect the Activity Log for that exact pair.

If a mapping is wrong or cannot be edited, send support the connection, source and destination variant IDs, SKUs, and a screenshot of the error. Do not create extra mappings to work around an incorrect pair; that can make the relationship harder to trace.
